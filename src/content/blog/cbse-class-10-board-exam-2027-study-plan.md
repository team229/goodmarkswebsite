<p>Sixteen weeks from the first week of November 2026 is enough time for the CBSE Class 10 board exams, if the hours are real. Plan two to two and a half focused hours on school days, one longer weekend block, and one rule most families break: read the marking scheme before you attempt the sample paper.</p>

<p><strong>Key takeaways</strong></p>

<ul>
  <li>Four phases in sixteen weeks, each ending with something you can hold.</li>
  <li>About fifteen hours a week, in two short evening blocks.</li>
  <li>Weeks one to fourteen carry the work. The last two are a buffer.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Are the 2027 sample papers out, and why does that change the plan?</h2>

<p>Yes. CBSE has published the Class X Sample Question Papers and Marking Schemes for the 2026-27 session, the one your child sits in: <a href="https://cbseacademic.nic.in/SQP_CLASSX_2026-27.html">Class X SQP and MS 2026-27 (CBSE)</a>. Every subject there carries a paper and a scheme.</p>

<p>An SQP is a full paper in the board's format: same sections, question types and time. Not the actual paper, not a forecast of which chapter turns up, and not a replacement for NCERT. From week one, you know what a long answer has to contain.</p>

<p>Schemes live on the board's <a href="https://www.cbse.gov.in/cbsenew/marking-scheme.html">marking scheme index (CBSE)</a>. Read the heading before you download: when we checked, the top of that page showed the 2026 second board schemes, not the 2026-27 ones. Take the 2027 files from the SQP page.</p>

<h2>What do the sixteen weeks actually look like?</h2>

<p>Count from the first week of November. The dates below are arithmetic, not a date sheet.</p>

<p>Week one starts on 2 November 2026; week fourteen ends on 7 February 2027. That is the date that matters: syllabus covered twice, every subject through one full paper. Week sixteen ends on 21 February.</p>

<p>CBSE has not published the 2027 date sheet, so nobody knows whether the first paper falls before that. Which is why phase four is short. If your first paper comes early, cut phase four, not phase three.</p>

<p>Phase one is the only one about finishing: the NCERT exercises attempted with the book closed. Name the weak chapters. Six names is a plan; "weak in Science" is a mood. The free <a href="/cbse-worksheets/">Class 10 Maths and Science worksheets</a> find them faster than re-reading.</p>

<p>Phase two dies quietly: syllabus done, tests weeks away, everyone coasts. Chapter-wise previous-year questions fix that. Phase three gets skipped because it feels like wasting paper, and it is the only part that rehearses the actual morning.</p>

<h2>What does a normal school week look like?</h2>

<p>Take a student who leaves at seven, gets home at four, has tuition two evenings, sport twice, and a sibling who needs dinner. They have two good hours, if the phone is elsewhere.</p>

<p>That is about fifteen hours, dropping to twelve some weeks. Twelve focused hours beat twenty-five distracted ones.</p>

<p>From week eleven, one weekday evening becomes a full paper instead of two blocks. Eight papers by 7 February; the other four sit in phase four, so every subject gets two.</p>

<h2>Where do marks actually leak, subject by subject?</h2>

<p>Parents hear "she's weak in Science". The marks leave in small, boring places, and they differ by subject.</p>

<p><strong>Mathematics.</strong> Step marks are the leak. The scheme pays for the formula line, the substitution and the unit, so a right answer with two lines of working can collect less than a wrong one showing the method. If the score has stalled, read <a href="/blogs/class-10-maths-harder-than-it-should/">why Class 10 Maths is harder than it should be</a>.</p>

<p>Basic and Standard are separate papers, and the old rule that Basic closed Class 11 Maths has changed. CBSE's <a href="https://www.cbse.gov.in/cbsenew/documents/Circular27052025_041.pdf">May 2025 circular</a> lets a Basic student offer Mathematics in Class 11 if the school head is satisfied about aptitude. The choice locks at registration.</p>

<p><strong>Science.</strong> One paper covering three subjects: Biology, Chemistry and Physics. Your child sits one Science exam, not three. The losses repeat: labels missing, units dropped, equations unbalanced. <a href="/blogs/physics-boards-sneak-up-on-class-10-students/">Physics in Class 10</a> loses marks in the working; <a href="/blogs/struggling-with-class-10-biology-before-boards/">Biology</a> in a diagram drawn too small to label.</p>

<p><strong>Social Studies.</strong> One paper covering four subjects: History, Geography, Political Science, Economics. The losses are dates, terms and map work. "In the 1920s" instead of a year drops marks that were in reach. Map questions sit in the History and Geography parts and are the cheapest marks on the paper.</p>

<p><strong>English.</strong> This is the subject parents most often get wrong. English (Language and Literature) is one subject, code 184, one paper, one marking scheme. It carries reading, grammar and creative writing, then literature questions. There is no separate language paper and no separate literature paper.</p>

<p>The two habits are real, but both live inside that paper. The language half rewards format and accuracy; the literature half rewards evidence, so a plot summary with no line from the text earns little. The confusion comes from another subject: English (Communicative) has its own paper, and some schools enter students for it.</p>

<p><strong>Hindi.</strong> Hindi A and B are separate papers; your school enters one. Grammar is where the quiet marks go: students read the chapter well, then lose marks in a grammar exercise never practised in writing.</p>

<h2>How do you use a marking scheme without wasting a paper?</h2>

<ol>
  <li><strong>Read the scheme before the paper.</strong> Download both together, read the section you are about to attempt, then close it. You want where the marks sit, not answers.</li>
  <li><strong>Note the steps that earn.</strong> In a numerical, the formula line, the substitution and the unit often carry marks separately. Write those three at the top.</li>
  <li><strong>Attempt under real time.</strong> No notes, no phone, no pause at ninety minutes.</li>
  <li><strong>Mark it yourself in a different colour</strong>, step by step, not answer by answer. Students skip this, and it is the only part that produces learning.</li>
  <li><strong>Log the error type, not the topic.</strong> Not "Trigonometry". Write "skipped the substitution line", "unit missing", "ran out of time on the long answers".</li>
</ol>

<p>One paper, one log page. By the third paper the same errors repeat, and that list beats any reference book bought in January.</p>

<p>Do not hand the paper to a chatbot to mark. It can explain a step. It cannot see which step you skipped at the eighty-minute mark.</p>

<h2>What should parents do, and what should they stop doing?</h2>

<p>Twenty minutes a week, notebook open. Three questions: what got marked in red, what is next week's list, what do you need from us. Then close it. Daily interrogation produces a child who reports progress instead of making it.</p>

<p>Protect sleep like the tuition slot. A fixed lights-out time in the last month beats one extra chapter, and nobody argues about it like they argue about marks.</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Stop buying reference books after December.</strong> A new guide in January gives a student a fresh reason to begin at page one.</li>
  <li><strong>Stop sitting beside the child to study.</strong> It works for a week, then the student cannot start without you.</li>
  <li><strong>Stop comparing to a cousin who scored well.</strong> It changes nothing about the paper.</li>
</ul>

<p>Ask the school how internal assessment is built; those marks come from schoolwork, not the board paper. If you are comparing centres, read <a href="/cbse-tuition-classes-gurgaon/">how CBSE tuition in Gurgaon is usually structured</a> first.</p>

<h2>When is tuition the right call, and what should you ask?</h2>

<p>Three signals, in order of seriousness. Answers are not corrected for format, so the same error survives every test. Doubts wait a week. And nobody has taught the student to mark a paper.</p>

<p>Then ask four things. Who marks the scripts, and can you see a marked one? When is doubt time, and is it in the timetable or the brochure? How much of the week is tests? How many hours are taught against practised?</p>

<h2>How does a sixteen-week plan fail?</h2>

<ol>
  <li><strong>Backlog denial.</strong> "We'll cover it in revision" is how one weak chapter becomes four.</li>
  <li><strong>Sample papers too early.</strong> A full paper in November tests chapters nobody has taught.</li>
  <li><strong>No written practice.</strong> Reading an answer and agreeing with it feels like studying. It is not.</li>
  <li><strong>All subjects every day.</strong> Six shallow starts and no finished chapter.</li>
  <li><strong>The phone in the study room.</strong> Not face down on the desk. In another room.</li>
</ol>
