<p>Twelve weeks from 12 November 2026 is enough for the CBSE Class 12 boards if the week is split on purpose: four phases with finish lines, from syllabus and practical file to pre-board repair, full papers, then a taper. Board work takes the first study slot daily; JEE or NEET work takes the second.</p>

<p><strong>Key takeaways</strong></p>

<ul>
  <li>Four phases from 12 November 2026 to 3 February 2027. The dates are arithmetic, not a CBSE schedule.</li>
  <li>The practical file closes in week three, not in February.</li>
  <li>Board work gets the first slot. Entrance work gets the second, daily.</li>
  <li>A pre-board is a diagnosis, and the repair list is the output.</li>
</ul>

<h2>What is different about Class 12 boards that Class 10 advice misses?</h2>

<p>Class 10 advice assumes one calendar; Class 12 runs two. CBSE publishes its date sheet on its own schedule, and JEE Main and NEET dates are announced separately.</p>

<p>The syllabus is less forgiving: a Class 11 chapter that never worked reappears inside a Class 12 derivation and an entrance numerical in the same month. <a href="/blogs/class-12-physics-doesnt-ease-you-in/">Class 12 Physics does not ease you in</a>.</p>

<p>Practicals are the second difference. In the sciences, three things get checked: the record, the project report and the viva. Build the file in February and the gaps show.</p>

<p>Third, the stream decides what doubles up. PCM students meet Physics, Chemistry and Maths in both papers; PCB students meet Physics, Chemistry and Biology.</p>

<p>Pre-boards eat three or four of the twelve weeks and land in the entrance panic.</p>

<h2>What does the 12-week plan look like, phase by phase?</h2>

<p>Weeks count from 12 November 2026. They are arithmetic, not a date sheet, and stop on 3 February 2027. If your pre-boards fall outside weeks five to eight, move phase two to meet them. If your first paper comes earlier, cut the taper, not the papers.</p>

<p>Phase one is the only phase about finishing, and it ends with a signature. Phase two is the one students skip, because a pre-board feels like a verdict. Phase four adds nothing new; that's the point.</p>

<p>Download the Class XII sample papers and marking schemes for 2026-27 from <a href="https://cbseacademic.nic.in/SQP_CLASSXII_2026-27.html">CBSE's academics unit</a> in week one, not week nine.</p>

<h2>How do you split a week between boards and JEE/NEET?</h2>

<p>A week has room a day doesn't, so the split is weekly.</p>

<p>About sixteen hours of self-study in a normal week, twelve when school tests and tuition collide. From week nine, two board slots become full papers: Saturday morning and one weekday evening. Paper weeks run to eighteen hours.</p>

<p>The first slot gets the good hour, so it can't be shared. Board work keeps it until the last paper. Entrance work takes the second slot daily, especially the days you feel behind, when it gets raided for a three-hour mock.</p>

<p>Inside the board slot, choose by deadline. Whatever has a school date goes first: a practical, a viva, a pre-board. If nothing has a date, take the one you avoided last week.</p>

<p>One exception: in an entrance exam week, that subject takes the first slot for six days. Then the order returns.</p>

<p>If Maths keeps losing its slot, the fix is a separate track. <a href="/blogs/maths-coaching-for-class-12-gurgaon/">Board and JEE Maths need different practice</a>.</p>

<h2>What about the practical file, project and viva?</h2>

<p>Close the file in week three. It needs a teacher's signature and a lab slot, and both get scarce once pre-boards begin.</p>

<p>"Closed" is not "written up":</p>

<ul>
  <li>The index matches the experiments and activities you actually performed, in order.</li>
  <li>Every observation is dated; every experiment and activity is signed.</li>
  <li>The project report is your own: aim, method, data, conclusion, sources.</li>
  <li>You can explain four experiments aloud, file closed.</li>
</ul>

<p>A borrowed project shows in the viva, when the examiner asks why you chose that method.</p>

<p>Internal assessment is school work: tests, notebook, practicals, whatever the teacher has recorded. It is mostly locked before the practical exam, so February is too late.</p>

<h2>How should pre-boards be used?</h2>

<p>As a diagnosis, not a judgement. The school marks the paper, so the number isn't comparable with a cousin's at another school. The pattern in your script is.</p>

<ol>
  <li><strong>Sit it as the real thing.</strong> Full time, no notes, the handwriting you'll use in the board hall.</li>
  <li><strong>Mark by question type, not by total.</strong> Count the one-liners, short answers, long answers, numericals, diagrams. The total hides the pattern.</li>
  <li><strong>Write four repair items per subject</strong>, ranked by marks lost, not by dislike.</li>
</ol>

<p>Repair is a new attempt at the same question type, marked again. Rereading the chapter is reassurance. The same failure twice is a signal, and that's when the timetable changes.</p>

<p>If you ran out of time, practise stopping rather than studying more. Most scripts we mark aren't short of knowledge. They're short of minutes.</p>

<p>Marking schemes are archived on <a href="https://www.cbse.gov.in/cbsenew/marking-scheme.html">CBSE's marking scheme index</a>. Check the session label: on 5 November 2026, the page opened on the 2026 supplementary schemes, not 2026-27. Take the current session from the SQP page.</p>

<h2>Why do good students lose marks in the answer booklet?</h2>

<p>Not in the chapter nobody revised. In presentation: small decisions under pressure, forty times.</p>

<p><strong>Step marks.</strong> The scheme pays for the formula line, the substitution and the unit, often on separate lines. A right answer buried in scribble can lose to a wrong one that shows method.</p>

<p><strong>Diagrams.</strong> Pencil, labels, large enough to read at arm's length. Arrows missing from a ray diagram make it a guess.</p>

<p><strong>Units.</strong> Physics loses units. Chemistry loses the condition above the arrow. Maths loses the step done in the head.</p>

<p><strong>First and last page.</strong> The first carries your name and numbering. The last carries the question everyone rushes.</p>

<p>An "e" that reads as "c" in a formula is a wrong answer. Handwriting is the cheapest leak to close.</p>

<h2>What should you stop doing in December?</h2>

<p>December decides the year by subtraction.</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Dropping board revision for mocks.</strong> A three-hour entrance mock before a pre-board buys a score you can't use.</li>
  <li><strong>Opening new entrance topics.</strong> A chapter started in December is revised badly and forgotten.</li>
  <li><strong>The all-nighter.</strong> It measures sleep, not preparation.</li>
  <li><strong>A new reference book.</strong> It restarts the syllabus at page one, in January.</li>
</ul>

<p>None of this is doing less. It's refusing to start what can't be finished by February.</p>

<h2>How do parents help without adding another timetable?</h2>

<p>Three things, and none of them is a new study plan.</p>

<p><strong>Ask for the marked script, not the score.</strong> A pre-board mark from another school tells you little. Ask what was marked, in what colour, and what the repair list says.</p>

<p><strong>Chase the signatures now.</strong> One unsigned page in December becomes a phone call in February. Ask the teacher what's pending, and write it down.</p>

<p><strong>Don't buy a new test series in December.</strong> It resets the syllabus clock, and a student holding a fresh stack will try to finish it.</p>
