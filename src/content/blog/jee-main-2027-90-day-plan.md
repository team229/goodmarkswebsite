<p>90 days to JEE Main Session 1 splits into three blocks of thirty. November is for diagnosis and covering what is half-finished. December converts knowledge into timed papers. January repairs logged errors and tapers. One full mock every Saturday, analysed every Sunday. What decides the last month is not new chapters. It is whether your error log actually changes what you study.</p>

<p><strong>Key takeaways</strong></p>

<ul>
  <li>Three blocks, not twelve identical weeks. The work changes every thirty days.</li>
  <li>One mock a week, marked and analysed, beats three rushed ones.</li>
  <li>The weakest subject gets the first slot of the day, never the last.</li>
  <li>From late December, nothing new enters the plan.</li>
</ul>

<p>NTA had not published a 2027 Session 1 date sheet when this was written, so the plan counts from 2 November. Watch <a href="https://jeemain.nta.nic.in/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">the official JEE Main portal</a> for the schedule and the information bulletin. When it appears, the blocks shift. Their order does not.</p>

<h2>Where do you actually stand on 2 November?</h2>

<p>Your last coaching test covered chapters you had just finished, and you have read the solutions. That measures a good day. You need a normal one.</p>

<p>Take a diagnostic this week: ninety minutes, thirty questions, ten per subject, from a paper you have not seen.</p>

<p>Mark it in three columns: right, wrong, blank. The blanks matter most. A wrong answer is a wrong method, which can be corrected. A blank is a missing method.</p>

<p>Score it twice: by section for where the marks went, by error type for why. One line per wrong or blank answer, naming the chapter and the failing step.</p>

<p>You end with one page of red and amber chapters. Twelve to fifteen of them own the first slot for eight weeks.</p>

<h2>The three 30-day blocks</h2>

<p>Most 90-day plans repeat one routine twelve times. That fails: covering a chapter and surviving a three-hour paper are different skills, and only one is built under a clock.</p>

<p>If the date sheet lands earlier than recent cycles have, cut a week from block two, never block three.</p>

<p>Block one is where students lose the ninety days. They start the hardest unfinished chapter on day one and live in it for three weeks. Finish the sixty-per-cent chapters first: six half-chapters made usable move a paper more than one difficult chapter mastered alone. A full-year structure handles it differently: <a href="/blogs/one-year-jee-coaching-gurgaon-plan/">how a one-year plan sequences its revision</a>.</p>

<h2>What does a normal week look like?</h2>

<p>Hours are a ceiling, not a target. A school-going student has about twenty usable hours a week. A dropper has forty-five, and wastes more, because a long day invites long breaks.</p>

<p>The mock never moves to make room for revision. A paper marked but not analysed is a three-hour rehearsal of habits you already have.</p>

<h2>How should the error log work?</h2>

<p>Every student has a log by December. Almost none change anything, because they read like diaries. “Did Q14 wrong, silly mistake, revise” cannot be acted on next Tuesday.</p>

<p>A working log separates errors by type, because each has a different repair. Five cover nearly everything:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Concept gap.</strong> You could not start, or chose the wrong principle. Repair: re-derive, then four fresh questions.</li>
  <li><strong>Recall gap.</strong> You knew the method and could not produce the formula or exception. Repair: spaced recall.</li>
  <li><strong>Execution.</strong> Right method, wrong algebra, sign or unit. Repair: a fixed checking habit.</li>
  <li><strong>Translation.</strong> You misread it. Missed “not”, “minimum” or a condition in the second line. Repair: underline the constraint first.</li>
  <li><strong>Time.</strong> Solvable, but you ran out, or spent six minutes on a two-minute question. Repair: a rule for when to leave.</li>
</ul>

<p>Every entry names the step that broke, not the topic. “Resolved tension wrong when the pulley had mass” is usable. “Pulleys weak” is not.</p>

<p>The rule: count types after every paper. One type three times, or one chapter in two consecutive papers, and next week changes. A slot is reassigned and something comes out. If nothing comes out, the plan was a wish list.</p>

<p>Re-solve logged questions after forty-eight hours, book closed. Fail twice and it goes to a teacher; <a href="/blogs/doubt-sessions-jee-neet-gurgaon/">a doubt slot used properly</a> settles in ten minutes what re-reading will not.</p>

<h2>How do you rotate subjects without losing one?</h2>

<p>The weakest subject usually gets the last hour, because a student can afford to lose it. It is also the hour cancelled when the day runs late, which is why it stays weak.</p>

<p>Reverse it. The weakest section gets the first slot, at whatever time you are actually awake.</p>

<p>Match the block to the subject. Physics and Mathematics need fifty to sixty minutes, because a problem abandoned halfway teaches nothing. Chemistry decays faster and splits into twenty-minute bursts.</p>

<p>So not a third each. If Mathematics is weak, it takes seventy-five minutes in the first slot daily, Physics four long blocks a week, Chemistry forty minutes in two pieces. If Chemistry is weak, two short slots every day.</p>

<p>Rotate the two stronger subjects. A subject you touch once a week is one you relearn every week.</p>

<p>Physics fails quietly: a student can carry electrostatics and still lose marks on the mechanics underneath. If the diagnostic shows that, <a href="/blogs/class-11-physics-hard-first-term/">Class 11 Physics that never got repaired</a> does not heal in December.</p>

<h2>What should you stop doing in the last three weeks?</h2>

<p><strong>New chapters.</strong> A chapter begun on 20 December will be half-learned on exam day, and takes the slot revision needed. Half-learned chapters produce wrong answers instead of blanks.</p>

<p><strong>A new book or test series.</strong> A fresh series in December means unfamiliar marking and three papers spent learning it. The papers you own, done twice, are worth more.</p>

<p><strong>All-nighters.</strong> One late night costs the next day's mock and the day after. Sleep debt feels exactly like a concept gap.</p>

<p><strong>Group study that becomes a rumour exchange.</strong> If the last forty minutes were about a teacher, a rank or a predicted percentile, it was not study.</p>

<p><strong>Dropping mocks to revise.</strong> The mock is how revision gets tested. Remove it and you revise blind.</p>

<p><strong>Percentile chatter.</strong> Predictions from coaching groups are guesses about a population you cannot see. They change nothing you do on Tuesday.</p>

<h2>What should the week of the exam look like?</h2>

<p>Boring. That is the goal. The week is for arriving with a steady routine and no new information.</p>

<p>Fix your wake time five days out and keep it. Move towards the exam's start time gradually, not with a 5am alarm. Sleep is the one input you cannot recover later.</p>

<p>Revision stays narrow: your own notes, the error log, formula sheets in your handwriting. Three to four hours a day. Ten hours in the last week mostly produces fatigue.</p>

<p>Settle logistics early: centre, travel time at that hour, a buffer. Print the admit card and check the identification requirement in the information bulletin, not a WhatsApp forward.</p>

<p>No new material after day three, no full mock after day two. The day before, nothing academic after lunch.</p>

<h2>What should parents do?</h2>

<p>Less than most do, and more precisely.</p>

<p>Protect sleep before study hours. A student sleeping six hours and studying eleven performs worse than one sleeping eight and studying nine. Set the lights-out time and hold it in January.</p>

<p>Hold one review a week. Fifteen minutes on Sunday, one question: “Which question could you not start?” That gets a real answer. “How much did you study?” gets a number.</p>

<p>Do not compare. A cousin's marks turn a method problem into a character problem, and papers stop being shown to you.</p>

<p>Do not buy a new test series in December. It is the season's most common expensive mistake, and it usually goes half-finished. If money is on your mind, ask <a href="/blogs/jee-coaching-fees-gurgaon-2026/">what coaching in Gurgaon actually costs</a>.</p>

<p>Do not sit in the room. A watched student avoids the questions they might get wrong.</p>
