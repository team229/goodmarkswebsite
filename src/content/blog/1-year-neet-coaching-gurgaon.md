<h2><strong>Why Twelve Months Feels So Short for NEET Specifically</strong></h2><br />

<p>NEET leans hard on Biology. Something like half the total marks come from it alone, with Physics and Chemistry splitting the rest and both still demanding real conceptual depth, not just formula memorization. Students walking into a one-year batch are usually one of two types, droppers who've already sat for NEET once, or Class 12 students who didn't take it seriously early enough and are now trying to catch up fast.</p><br />

<p>Do the subtraction. Board prep eats a chunk of the year. The actual exam period eats more. What's left is realistically eight, maybe nine months of usable study time. Not twelve. That's the whole reason a properly built 1 year NEET coaching Gurgaon batch teaches nothing like a two-year program would. There's no room for slow, exploratory teaching here. Everything has to be tight.</p><br />

<h2><strong>What Actually Separates a Working Plan From a Rushed One</strong></h2><br />

<p>High-yield NCERT Biology chapters, the ones NEET keeps pulling questions from year after year, need to get tackled early. Early enough that there's still time to revise them more than once before the exam. Physics and Chemistry work best with a similar logic, numerical-heavy topics up front, before the fatigue of a long study year starts creeping in.</p><br />

<p>Weekly full-length NEET-pattern tests matter more than chapter-end quizzes ever will. Cumulative testing is really what builds recall under actual exam pressure, not just familiarity with a topic in isolation. And some chapters simply carry more NEET weightage than others (that's just a fact of the exam pattern), so time should go where the marks actually are, not spread out evenly like it's a school syllabus.</p><br />

<p>Revision, too, needs to start early. Not get crammed into the last sixty days out of sheer panic, which is honestly what happens in a lot of coaching setups that don't plan ahead.</p><br />

<p>Not really, no. It tends to work best for droppers who already carry a decent base from a previous attempt and mostly need refinement, not a full restart. Class 12 students with reasonable fundamentals do well with it too, since they need a focused push more than a ground-up rebuild. And students who genuinely perform better under a fast, test-heavy rhythm rather than a slower pace tend to thrive in this kind of setup.</p><br />

<p>If someone's starting from genuinely weak fundamentals though, a one-year plan can still work.
