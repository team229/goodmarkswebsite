<p>Choosing a coaching institute in Gurgaon comes down to ten checks you can run in one visit: who teaches, the real headcount, how a doubt reaches a teacher, who marks the scripts, test review, the fee sheet, batch switching, the catch-up plan, the named person and the trial class. The decisive one is who marks the scripts. Ask for a marked page with the name covered.</p>

<p><strong>Key takeaways</strong></p>

<ul>
  <li>The brochures say the same things: experienced faculty, small batches, regular tests, personal attention. These checks turn each phrase into something you can count.</li>
  <li>Who marks the work matters more than who lectures. A tick is not feedback.</li>
  <li>Batch size tells you how crowded the room is, not whether anyone reads the work.</li>
  <li>None of this guarantees a result. It tells you whether the system notices.</li>
</ul>

<h2>What do the ten checks actually test?</h2>

<p>The sentences sound alike by the third visit: experienced faculty, small batches, regular tests, personal attention. None of it is false. None of it can be checked either.</p>

<p>All ten test one thing: does anyone here read your child's work and act on it?</p>

<p>The early answers blur together. The marked page is where they part.</p>

<h2>Who teaches the batch, and will they stay?</h2>

<p>Ask for the names of the three subject teachers for your child's batch. Not the faculty page. The people in the room on a Tuesday.</p>

<p>Then ask who teaches that batch next year. A centre that hands every batch to a new teacher in April is running two one-year courses and calling it a two-year course. The <a href="/course/2-year-integrated-regular/">two-year integrated programme</a> is where that question matters most.</p>

<p>Ask about turnover. "How many teachers left mid-year?" One is normal. A pause and "we have a large faculty pool" means the batch is taught by whoever is free.</p>

<h2>What is the real batch size?</h2>

<p>A cap is a promise about next month. A count is a fact about Tuesday.</p>

<p>Count heads twenty minutes into a running class. If the centre will not let you sit in, wait at the door as a batch ends and count the students who leave. Then ask the teacher at the door how many are enrolled. If the numbers disagree, believe the room.</p>

<p>Ask whether the cap is per section and includes students who joined after the first test. A centre that opens a section whenever demand rises has a target, not a cap.</p>

<p>Thirty students at ten minutes each is five hours of attention. Nobody has that, so attention comes from marked scripts, doubt slots and one weekly check. Batch size measures crowding, not attention.</p>

<p>Online batches can be smaller. <a href="/blogs/online-vs-offline-jee-coaching-gurgaon/">Online vs offline JEE coaching in Gurgaon</a> covers the other differences.</p>

<h2>How does a doubt reach a teacher, and when?</h2>

<p>Every centre advertises doubt clearing. The question is what happens to a doubt raised on a Tuesday.</p>

<p>Ask three things in order: which channel, who reads it, when it is answered. A slip, a WhatsApp group or a named desk can all work. "Anytime" cannot. Anytime is permission, not a route. A route has a name, a place and a day.</p>

<p>Ask what happens if the doubt is not solved in the slot. A good answer carries it forward or moves it to a one-to-one session. The weak one is a shrug.</p>

<p>A weekly slot is a backstop, not a route. The class moves on before the answer arrives. <a href="/blogs/doubt-sessions-jee-neet-gurgaon/">How doubt sessions actually work</a> sets out the questions that expose a weak one.</p>

<h2>Who marks the scripts?</h2>

<p>This one decides most cases. Ask: "Can I see a page from a recent test, with the name covered?" Not a topper's sheet. One from the middle of the batch.</p>

<p>Then read it. A tick says the answer was seen. A mark says where it lost marks. Look for the step that was cut and a correction in words. If the page holds a tick and a total, nobody has told the student what to change.</p>

<p>A refusal on privacy grounds is reasonable. Ask the teacher to blank the name and roll number, or to walk you through one recent script. A subject teacher within a week is useful. An evaluator three weeks later is an audit, not feedback.</p>

<p>CBSE awards step marks. JEE Main awards the final answer, with negative marking for a wrong MCQ. A centre teaching both should mark both differently. Ask which rubric produced the page in your hand.</p>

<p>If nobody can produce a marked page in ten minutes, there's your answer. Marking is not in the system. It's what a teacher does when there is time.</p>

<p>A marked page does not promise a rank. It shows whether an adult watches the mistakes.</p>

<h2>How are tests used after the paper comes back?</h2>

<p>Frequency is the easy answer and the least useful. Ask what happens after the paper.</p>

<p>Invigilated, same room, same timing, no phone: that's a test. A paper taken home is homework with a score attached.</p>

<p>Ask who sets it. A centre's own paper follows its teaching; a bought test series gives a national benchmark.</p>

<p>The review is where the value sits. An error log is better than a batch discussion: date, question, why it went wrong, what to do next. Ask to see one real page. If the review is not in the timetable, it happens in the corridor.</p>

<p>Ask whether board papers are separate from JEE and NEET papers. One paper cannot train both a step mark and a negative-marked guess.</p>

<h2>What should the fee sheet contain?</h2>

<p>Ask for the fee sheet as a document, not a number.</p>

<p>It should carry the total, hours per week, and what is included: material, test series, registration. Then the refund clause, the repeat-year policy and the rule for joining mid-year.</p>

<p>Compare the two documents line by line, not the two totals. If one sheet has no hours, the comparison is already over.</p>

<p>Ask what a repeat year costs if the first attempt goes badly. Get it in writing now. A counsellor who says "full refund" but cannot find the line is describing a habit, not a policy.</p>

<p>Two guides carry the published market numbers: <a href="/blogs/jee-coaching-fees-gurgaon-2026/">what JEE coaching in Gurgaon costs</a> and <a href="/blogs/neet-coaching-fees-gurgaon-2027/">NEET coaching fees for 2027</a>.</p>

<h2>What happens when a student falls behind?</h2>

<p>Ask as a story: "My daughter scores 30 in the first test in August. What happens in the next two weeks?"</p>

<p>A good answer has a name, a date and a plan: a backup class, an extra doubt slot, a call home with the paper, a re-test on the same chapter. A weak one is "we will inform you", or a mentor system with no meeting scheduled.</p>

<p>Ask about switching before you need it. Can a student move to a slower batch in October without paying again? "We will see" is a warning. A flat no means the batch is the product and the student fits it or leaves.</p>

<p>Then ask who calls. A role and a name. If attendance drops in September and nobody calls by October, the tracking is a dashboard, not a person.</p>

<h2>What should you do in one visit?</h2>

<p>Sixty minutes, one weekday, one child. Not an open day.</p>

<p>Three questions do not fit the clock: the test review, the batch switch and the catch-up plan. Ask them in the same visit, before the fee sheet.</p>

<p>Take your child. A student notices whether the teacher waits after the question.</p>

<p>Do not decide the same day. A centre that pushes you to pay today is selling, not advising.</p>
