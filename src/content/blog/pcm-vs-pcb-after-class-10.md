<p>PCM keeps JEE Main open for B.E. and B.Tech because those courses require Physics and Mathematics. PCB keeps NEET UG open because it requires Biology. Neither keeps both open. The question to settle before the form: which exam can this student realistically prepare for across two years, given their marks and the timetable the school will actually run?</p>

<p><strong>Key takeaways</strong></p>

<ul>
  <li>Two subject rules decide this. Everything else is preference.</li>
  <li>PCMB keeps both open and charges a fourth subject's hours. It suits evidence, not indecision.</li>
  <li>The useful evidence is in the house: two terms of scripts, the homework finished unasked, the school's grid.</li>
  <li>Switching is cheap in week two and expensive in December. Ask what your school allows before you sign.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Which subject rule actually decides it?</h2>

<p><strong>JEE Main.</strong> The National Testing Agency's <a href="https://nta.ac.in/Engineeringexam">engineering exam page</a> lists the Class 12 subjects for B.E. and B.Tech: Physics and Mathematics as compulsory, plus one of Chemistry, Biotechnology, Biology or a technical vocational subject. Mathematics is not negotiable. Biology is only one option for the third slot, which is why a student with Physics, Mathematics and Biology is eligible for engineering while a student with Physics, Chemistry and Biology is not eligible through this route.</p>

<p><strong>NEET UG.</strong> The requirement, published in NTA's <a href="https://neet.nta.nic.in/">NEET (UG) information bulletin</a> and restated each year, is Physics, Chemistry, Biology or Biotechnology, and English. No Mathematics.</p>

<p>So PCM without Biology is not eligible for NEET UG, and PCB without Mathematics is not eligible for JEE Main for B.E. or B.Tech. Neither stream is better. Each is an eligibility set with a hole in it, and the hole sits in a different place.</p>

<p>Both holes can be closed by adding the missing subject. That's a school decision, not a family one. The subject has to be timetabled, taught, and printed on the Class 12 mark statement. A vague answer is a no. Programme comparisons come later: <a href="/blogs/iit-jee-coaching-gurgaon/">JEE coaching in Gurgaon</a> and <a href="/blogs/neet-coaching-gurgaon/">NEET coaching in Gurgaon</a> both assume eligibility is settled.</p>

<h2>What does PCMB really cost?</h2>

<p>PCMB looks like the safe answer, and on paper it is: four subjects, both exams open, no door shut at fifteen. The bill arrives weekly, as time.</p>

<p>Four subjects against three is roughly a quarter more class time and homework, plus an extra paper each test cycle. The larger cost is two revision plans in the same fortnight.</p>

<p>Something gives, and it is the same thing each year: one subject is prepared for the board, not the entrance exam. That can be a sensible plan. It's a bad accident. Name the subject in July and tell its teacher.</p>

<p>It suits a narrow group. Maths and Biology marks close and both earned without heavy tutoring. A student who finishes set work unasked. A school that runs the combination without a clash on the timetable grid.</p>

<h2>What evidence should you use instead of opinions?</h2>

<p>It looks like a decision that needs a counsellor and a two-hour test. It takes four things already in the house.</p>

<p><strong>Read scripts, not percentages.</strong> A 78 in Maths built on the familiar sections and a 72 in Biology built on diagrams are not comparable numbers. Look at what was left blank, and whether the work was done alone. The Class 9 annual paper and this year's half-yearly are enough. If the Class 10 paper itself needs repair before the boards, the <a href="/blogs/cbse-class-10-board-exam-2027-study-plan/">Class 10 board study plan</a> covers that separately.</p>

<p><strong>Watch what gets finished on a Tuesday.</strong> PCM's week is short problems, most failing on the first attempt. PCB's week is volume: NCERT pages, diagrams, definitions, then the same pages again. Some students sit with one sum for twenty minutes. Others read forty pages and produce clean notes. Both are real work, and different temperaments. Two years is long to spend doing the kind that never becomes natural.</p>

<p><strong>Ask for the grid.</strong> Not the subject list, the timetable: which periods, which teacher, which lab slot, and whether the fourth subject lands at the end of the day. This is the check families skip. A relative's certainty is not evidence either. Nor is a 40-minute aptitude test that has never seen the Maths homework.</p>

<h2>What do students get wrong in the first month?</h2>

<p><strong>Choosing the friend's combination.</strong> Sections fill up, friends land together, and the choice turns social. The friend will not be in the hall in two years. Ask the student to explain the decision to a teacher without naming anyone else.</p>

<p><strong>Ignoring a Maths gap.</strong> Class 11 Mathematics builds on Class 10 algebra, trigonometry and coordinate geometry within weeks, so a shaky base shows up as a slow start, not a dramatic failure. The repair is twenty minutes a day on the old chapters, not a change of stream. Physics is the subject all three combinations share: <a href="/blogs/class-11-physics-hard-first-term/">why Class 11 Physics feels impossible by November</a>.</p>

<p><strong>Treating Biology as memorisation.</strong> Volume is real and so is the trap. NEET UG Biology asks for application inside familiar content, and a student who can recite a line without placing it in a situation loses marks they believe they have studied for.</p>

<p><strong>Assuming PCMB means four subjects prepared equally.</strong> Four at full intensity for two years is a schedule nobody sustains. Decide in July which subject is carried at board level, and say it out loud.</p>

<h2>What if the student changes their mind in Class 11?</h2>

<p>It happens. The cost depends on when.</p>

<p><strong>In the first two or three weeks, it is a timetable change.</strong> Schools can move a student while the syllabus is near the start, and that window closes quietly. Ask on day one what the window is, in writing. The answer in July is friendlier than the answer in December.</p>

<p><strong>After a term, it is a syllabus problem.</strong> Class 11 Mathematics is sequential: sets and functions under trigonometry, trigonometry under calculus. A student joining in November has missed the run-up, not one chapter. Biology has the same shape: a term of terminology later chapters assume.</p>

<p><strong>The additional-subject route is worth asking about and worth doubting.</strong> Schools sometimes allow a sixth subject in Class 11 or 12, and it can appear on the mark statement. Whether an admitting body counts it for a particular course is their answer, not the school's.</p>

<h2>What should parents ask the school before signing?</h2>

<p>Five questions. The answers beat any brochure.</p>

<ol>
  <li><strong>Which combinations are running this year, and how many sections of each?</strong> A combination printed in the prospectus with four students in it may not run.</li>
  <li><strong>Who teaches Class 11 Mathematics and Biology, and will the same teacher take the batch into Class 12?</strong> Both build across the two years.</li>
  <li><strong>Can I see the Class 11 timetable for our combination?</strong> Look for clashes, where the fourth subject sits, and whether Biology practicals have a real lab period.</li>
  <li><strong>If we choose PCMB, which subject does the school treat as the additional one?</strong> Ask how it appears on the report card.</li>
  <li><strong>Can a subject be changed in the first month, and how?</strong> A verbal yes in the admission meeting is not a policy.</li>
</ol>

<p>If PCMB is on the table, ask what happened to last year's group: how many started, finished, and which subject went board-only.</p>
